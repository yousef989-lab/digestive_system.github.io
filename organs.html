The Main Organs Of The Digestive System

The mouth is the first part of the digestive system. It does both physical and chemical breakdown of the food Function: The mouth contains two main parts for breaking down food: the teeth and saliva. It is what starts the long process of digestion. When food enters the mouth, the saliva starts breaking it down immediately alongside the teeth. The saliva contains water, mucus, and salivary amylase. The mucus and water soften the food while the salivary amylase breaks down the starches into basic sugars. The teeth contribute to breaking down the food by cutting, tearing, and grinding to increase surface area and decrease the workload needed by the other organs later on.
The esophagus connects the pharynx and the stomach. It is located in front of the spinal column in the neck and behind the trachea, passing right behind the left lung and piercing the diaphragm.

Function: The esophagus transports food to the stomach for digestion. First, the food enters through the oral cavity and becomes bolus after being physically and chemically digested. The epiglottis covers the larynx to prevent the bolus from interrupting and irritating the respiratory system. The upper esophageal sphincter relaxes, allowing the bolus to enter the esophagus. The sphincter then closes soon after to make sure the bolus dosen't come back up. The esophagus then uses a wave-like muscle to move the bolus down by contracting the muscles behind the food and relaxing the muscles ahead, this is called peritalsis. Lastly, the lower esophageal sphincter relaxes to allow the bolus to enter the stomach before quickly contracting to prevent stomach acid from escaping. This process usually takes around 8 to 10 seconds.
The stomach is where bolus heads to from the esophagus. It is connected directly to the small intestine and neighbors the pancreas and liver Function: The stomach is the primary place of digestion. It uses stomach acid that consists of hydrochloric acid, a bacteria killer and pepsin, a protein decomposer that is used to further digest the bolus and turn it into a chyme before getting passed on to the small intestine.
The small intestine is connected to the stomach and the large intestine. Function: The small intestine is the organ responsible for absorbing nutrients into the bloodstream. It does this by using enzymes to break down carbohydrates, protiens, and fats, and diffusing them into small blood vessels called capillaries. These blood vessels are hidden inside the villi. The villi are little folds in the small intestine that help increase the surface area for the nutrients' transportation to the blood.
The cecum, appendix, colon, rectum and anal canal are all parts included in the large intestine. The colon alone has three sections: ascending colon, transverse colon, and descending colon.

Function: The large intestine receives the mostly digested food from the small intestine, then extracts some nutrients and all of the water as it prepares the waste for excretion.

The liver has so many jobs; it is one of the most important organs in the body. The liver produces bile that helps break down fat present in the small intestine. It also processes and stores minerals and nutrients like vitamin A, D, B12, and other minerals like iron absorbed from the chyme in the small intestine. Finally, it also removes toxins from the bloodstream like alchohol and drugs. Although overworking the liver (like excessive use of alchohol) can damage it, often long-term damage or persistent inflammation. This is known as cirrhosis.
