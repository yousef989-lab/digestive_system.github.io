The Main Organs Of The Digestive System

The esophagus connects the pharynx and the stomach. It is located in front of the spinal column in the neck and behind the trachea, passing right behind the left lung and piercing the diaphragm.

Function: The esophagus transports food to the stomach for digestion. First, the food enters through the oral cavity and becomes bolus after being physically and chemically digested. The epiglottis covers the larynx to prevent the bolus from interrupting and irritating the respiratory system. The upper esophageal sphincter relaxes, allowing the bolus to enter the esophagus. The sphincter then closes soon after to make sure the bolus dosen't come back up. The esophagus then uses a wave-like muscle to move the bolus down by contracting the muscles behind the food and relaxing the muscles ahead, this is called peritalsis. Lastly, the lower esophageal sphincter relaxes to allow the bolus to enter the stomach before quickly contracting to prevent stomach acid from escaping. This process usually takes around 8 to 10 seconds.
The cecum, appendix, colon, rectum and anal canal are all parts included in the large intestine. The colon alone has three sections: ascending colon, transverse colon, and descending colon.

Function: The large intestine receives the mostly digested food from the small intestine, then extracts some nutrients and all of the water as it prepares the waste for excretion.
