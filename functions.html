The Digestive System's Functions

  1. Digestion
    1. Mechanical Digestion:
      • The physical breakdown of food into smaller chunks through the act of chewing (mouth) and the process where the smooth muscles in the stomach wall contract and relax, mixing food with the gastric acids and enzymes present.
    2. Chemical Digestion:
      • Using the enzymes, acids, and water contained in your stomach to break down the food into molecular components (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, etc) that the body can then benefit from.
  2. Absorption
    • Once the nutrients get broken down, they are absorbed, primarily in the small intestine (although some of the absorption may also occur in the large intestine), and pass through the villi in the small intestine into the bloodstream. The nutrient-rich blood now circulates throughout the body.
  3. Elimination (waste removal):
    • The last function of the digestive system is to remove the remaining leftover waste product from the body. This is done by transporting the waste to the rectum from the large intestine where it will stay until it's time for its excretion.
